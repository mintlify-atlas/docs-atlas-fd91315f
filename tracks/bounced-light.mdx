Bounced light is CSP's system for producing subtle ground-level glow caused by trackside lights reflecting off the road surface and run-off areas. Instead of relying purely on direct illumination from `LIGHT_X` or `LIGHT_SERIES_X`, CSP builds a top-down light map of the track at runtime, then uses that map to create soft ambient fill in the low-angle areas around lit fixtures. On a night lap, bounced light is responsible for the characteristic orange or white haze you see creeping across the tarmac between lamp posts — making the surface feel genuinely lit rather than simply less dark.

Enabling and tuning bounced light requires you to categorise the materials in your track's KN5 by their role: which surfaces bounce light back upward, which objects should be excluded entirely, and which should cast shadows on the ground.

<Tip>
  Start with `SUPPORTED = 1` and leave all material lists empty. CSP will apply
  a default bounce to the whole scene. Drive a lap, then progressively refine
  `SURFACE_MATERIALS`, `OBJECT_MATERIALS`, and `OCCLUDING_MATERIALS` to remove
  artefacts and improve accuracy. It is much easier to subtract than to add from
  scratch.
</Tip>

## `[BOUNCED_LIGHT]` Parameters

<ParamField path="SUPPORTED" type="0 or 1" default="0">
  Master switch. Set to `1` to enable bounced light for this track. If this
  is `0` (or the section is absent), CSP skips all bounced-light processing
  for the circuit regardless of the other settings.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="SURFACE_MATERIALS" type="string (comma-separated)">
  List of material names that **receive and reflect** light back upward.
  These are the surfaces you want to show the ground glow — typically the
  racing surface, run-off areas, grass, painted kerbs, sand traps, and
  similar flat or near-flat surfaces. Supports `?` wildcards.

  Materials not listed here are treated as transparent to the light map
  and will not contribute a bounce reflection, so leaving this list empty
  while `SUPPORTED = 1` lets CSP use a built-in default.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="OBJECT_MATERIALS" type="string (comma-separated)">
  Materials to **completely exclude** from the light map. Add any upright or
  vertical geometry here — tyre barriers, jersey barriers, Armco rails,
  billboards, advertising boards, fencing, walls, and any other structures
  that should neither bounce nor occlude the ground glow. Supports `?`
  wildcards.

  Leaving objects like tall walls in the light map can cause incorrect
  bounced-light spread because their horizontal extent inflates the map.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="SPOT_MATERIALS" type="string (comma-separated)">
  Materials that **contribute** to the bounced light (like `SURFACE_MATERIALS`)
  but are **not used** to determine the ground height for the light map. Use
  this for rocks, kerb lips, mounds, or other raised features that naturally
  receive and reflect light but whose height should not distort the map's
  elevation baseline.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="OCCLUDING_MATERIALS" type="string (comma-separated)">
  Materials rendered as solid black in the light map, casting hard shadows
  on the ground beneath them. Use for significant overhead structures that
  genuinely block light from reaching the tarmac: bridges, pit lane roofs,
  grandstand canopies, and large signage gantries. Supports `?` wildcards.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="CLIP_POINT_X" type="x,y,z">
  A world-space coordinate that is used to clip the boundaries of the bounced-
  light map. On circuits with elevation changes or complex layouts, the
  automatic map boundary can extend into underground tunnels, basement areas,
  or far-away geometry that produces incorrect bounce contributions. Each
  `CLIP_POINT_X` (where `X` is a sequential integer starting from `0`) defines
  a point that clips the map to exclude geometry beyond it. You can add as many
  as needed.
</ParamField>

## Finding Material Names

To populate the material lists accurately you need to know the exact material names used in the track's KN5 file. Several tools can help:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Use CM's AC Model Viewer">
    Content Manager's built-in model viewer (right-click a KN5 in the
    `content/tracks/` folder → **View in AC Model Viewer**) displays every
    material and mesh name. Click on geometry in the viewport to see which
    material it uses.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Use 3DS Max or Blender KN5 importers">
    Importing the KN5 into a DCC application with an Assetto Corsa importer
    (e.g. the Blender AC plugin) gives you the full hierarchy. Material names
    appear exactly as they do in the INI.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Look at existing configs for the same track">
    If a track already has a CSP config in the repository, check its
    `SURFACE_MATERIALS` and `OBJECT_MATERIALS` lists — these are your best
    starting reference.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check neighbouring config files for naming patterns">
    Kunos tracks follow consistent naming conventions (`asph?`, `roads?`,
    `grass?`, `top1?`, etc.) which you can use as a starting template and
    refine from there.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Examples

### Minimal Setup — Enable and let CSP decide

```ini
[BOUNCED_LIGHT]
SUPPORTED = 1
```

### Red Bull Ring

A typical Kunos track setup — surface materials use `?` wildcards to cover
variant names, and `SUPPORTED = 1` is the only required key:

```ini
[BOUNCED_LIGHT]
SUPPORTED         = 1
SURFACE_MATERIALS = grass?, TOP, roads?, top1?, travert, sand, asph?, whiteline?, cnc_box, paint, Roof
```

### Brands Hatch

This example adds explicit occluding geometry and comments out the clip points
(they can be uncommented if the light map extends incorrectly into the valley):

```ini
[BOUNCED_LIGHT]
SUPPORTED            = 1
SURFACE_MATERIALS    = ?grs?, ?gras?, ?grass?, sbanc?, asph?, curbs?, concrete_green, concrete_box, sand, Asph?
; OBJECT_MATERIALS  = bridge?, occ
OCCLUDING_MATERIALS  = barrier, jersey1, grail, grail-final, dist, brake_sign, concrete_old, structures_alpha, LOGOS, soppalco, gstand_base, building2_main, direction, jersey, barrier_entrance
; CLIP_POINT_0      = 123.76, 2.75, 6.95
; CLIP_POINT_1      = 153.34, -3.63, -191.85
; CLIP_POINT_2      = -277.9, 4.35, -17.52
```

### Forli Ring (Elevation Changes Requiring Clip Points)

On hillside or multi-level circuits, clip points prevent the light map from
covering underground or far-off geometry. Each `CLIP_POINT_X` is a world-space
coordinate that helps CSP determine where the valid light-map area ends:

```ini
[BOUNCED_LIGHT]
SUPPORTED    = 1
CLIP_POINT_0 = -670.1,  199.64, -212.35
CLIP_POINT_1 = -785.02, 213.15,   13.75
CLIP_POINT_2 = -108.26, 179.08,  -63.28
CLIP_POINT_3 =  873.37, 109.07, -677.42
CLIP_POINT_4 = -211.2,  157.78, -490.95
CLIP_POINT_5 = -647.65, 134.0,  -840.22
CLIP_POINT_6 = -681.24, 200.22, -218.38
```

### Nordschleife (Large Track with Object Exclusion)

Long tracks often need `OBJECT_MATERIALS` to strip out trees, fencing, and
spectator areas that would incorrectly appear in the light map and produce
artificial glow patterns in the forest sections:

```ini
[BOUNCED_LIGHT]
SUPPORTED        = 1
OBJECT_MATERIALS = tree_shadow, branch?, fence?, grail?, marshall, jersey, ?house?, ?hotel?, tent?, TV_Stuff
CLIP_POINT_0     = -2542.08,  42.57,   -11.57
CLIP_POINT_1     =  -476.46, 141.38,  2364.61
CLIP_POINT_2     =   173.12, 111.08,  1733.29
```

## Common Issues and Solutions

<Warning>
  If you see bounced light appearing inside tunnels, buildings, or on the
  underside of bridges, add the relevant overhead structures to
  `OCCLUDING_MATERIALS` or `OBJECT_MATERIALS` and add `CLIP_POINT_X` values
  near the problem area.
</Warning>

<Warning>
  If the bounce glow looks too bright or bleeds onto walls and fencing, move
  those material names from `SURFACE_MATERIALS` to `OBJECT_MATERIALS`. Vertical
  surfaces should generally not be in the surface list.
</Warning>

<Tip>
  `SPOT_MATERIALS` is ideal for raised kerbs, rumble strips, or trackside
  boulders — they should glow under lights like the road surface, but their
  elevated position should not mislead the height calculation used to place the
  light map. Adding them to `SPOT_MATERIALS` rather than `SURFACE_MATERIALS`
  gives you the visual bounce without the height artefact.
</Tip>
